## Full description (both stores)

Written to the research in `RESEARCH.md` (same folder): the first three
lines carry the whole pitch; one short block per real feature after that.

> Your whole LinkedIn week, on one board.
>
> Open the app and see what needs you today: comments to answer, leads
> to follow up, freebies to send. No digging through LinkedIn to find
> your own list.
>
> HOME — WHAT NEEDS YOU
> Today's list first: comments waiting, follow-ups due, requests to
> send, plus your latest alerts. One screen, nothing missed.
>
> POSTS — PLANNED AND COUNTED
> Your posts for the week, written and scheduled in one list. Each
> post shows its real count of comments and likes — the numbers
> LinkedIn gives, counted honestly, never padded.
>
> OUTREACH — CUSTOMERS IN ORDER
> Your customer list in clear stages, from first invite to booked
> call. Open a name and see the next step, with a message draft
> written for that person, ready to send.
>
> FREEBIES — ASKED, SENT, DONE
> Every freebie request lands in one queue. Send it, tick it off,
> move on.
>
> PHOTO VAULT — YOUR BRAND PHOTOS
> The photos you post, kept in one place, one tap from the next post.
> Reminder notifications are available if you turn them on.
>
> WORKERS — THE WEEKLY JOBS
> The regular jobs behind your LinkedIn, each with its day and its
> instructions, so the week runs the same way every week.
>
> Your board saves to your own private workspace and syncs between
> your phone and your computer. Nothing is ever posted for you —
> you send every message yourself. The app never shows impressions
> or reach figures that LinkedIn does not provide.

## App preview video — decision

**No preview video at launch.** Full evidence in `RESEARCH.md`: the
pro-video numbers are vendor claims without a cited study, Apple's one
published number points the other way, and guidance for a text-and-
lists app is explicit — skip the video, invest in the frames. If a
preview is produced later for an A/B test, the storyboard is:

1. (0–4s) Home opens. Caption: "Your whole LinkedIn week."
2. (4–9s) The "Needs you" list ticks off. Caption: "See what needs you."
3. (9–15s) Posts list with comment and like counts. Caption: "Posts, planned and counted."
4. (15–21s) Outreach list; one lead opens; next step shows. Caption: "Every lead has a next step."
5. (21–26s) Freebie queue; one request marked sent. Caption: "Freebies, handled."
6. (26–30s) Photo vault grid. Caption: "Your photos, one tap away."

Rules: real screens only, silent (previews autoplay muted), captions
carry the words, first frame doubles as the poster frame.

## Content rating notes

- No user-generated public content, no chat with strangers, no ads, no
  gambling, no violent or sexual content, no location tracking.
- Google Play IARC questionnaire: answer "No" to every content category;
  the app is a private productivity tool. Expected rating: **Everyone**.
- Apple age rating questionnaire: "None" for every category in the
  standard matrix. Expected rating: **4+**.
- Apple "App Privacy" and Google Play "Data safety" answers must match
  `privacy-policy.html` in this folder: data is the owner's own business
  records in her own database; photos she uploads; a push endpoint if she
  enables reminders. Nothing is sold. No third-party advertising or
  analytics SDKs are included.
